# Agent Note: the plan-card shadow review group

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-17-feishu-bridge-plan-shadow.zh.md)

## Problem

A plan card parks its turn until the user answers, and the parked window is the cheapest moment to ask for a second opinion: a note on the card already rides the verdict into `exit_plan_mode`'s rejection path, so a user who pushes once more ("还有没有更好的、更鲁棒的、更优雅的方案？") may collect a better plan. Doing that by hand spends the plan they had — the same conversation re-plans and its card is gone — and the push itself is a manual step nobody repeats reliably.

Automating it needs a second conversation seeded from the same transcript, which the bridge could already build (`/fk`), plus two things it did not own:

- The plan-card emission had no post-park hook. `feishuBridge/ask-parked` fires only for question asks; the plan path ends at `deliverCards` with nothing an owner could listen to.
- Two such conversations had no way to settle each other. Whichever plan the user approves must void the other, and nothing linked the pair.

Linking them is not enough on its own: an approval is one way to be done with a plan card, and it is the rarer one. A user who answers a card by typing, declines it, or answers a question in the review group has decided something the peer keeps waiting on — the review keeps burning its way through a plan nobody is waiting for, or the origin card stays parked for a verdict that no longer decides anything, and cleaning the peer up is a manual `/done` that also takes the peer's child groups down.

## Decision

`engine/plan-shadow.ts` owns the feature behind three entries: `Engine.askUser`'s plan-review branch (a parked card was approved), `Engine.handleInbound`, the ingress every platform-delivered human action passes (`handleMessage` itself stays the shared path for synthetic injections), and the tail of `Engine.handleResultEvent`, where a shadow turn that parked no plan card retires its own group.

**Launch.** Right after the plan card lands and the ask parks — after the `deliverCards` race, before the decision race — `launchPlanShadow` runs fire-and-forget, so group creation never sits between the parked card and the user's approval window. The shadow is a `/fk`-shaped child: `spawnGroupCommon` with `forkSentinelID = __fork__<nativeID>`, `modeArg: 'plan'`, and the review prompt as the injected first message. The child is a fresh group on the origin chat's working directory, provider route, and membership; no worktree, because a plan-mode session does not touch the tree.

**Guards** (all in `launchableSession`, which hands the launch its origin session and answers every skip with a silent `undefined`): the per-project switch, a plan with text, a group-capable platform, a session that exists, a forkable native session id (started, neither `__fork__` nor `__forkat__`), a spawn user that is neither empty nor the cron synthetic sender — the shadow group's only member is that user, so a group invited around `cron` would be one nobody can open, let alone approve — not itself a shadow, and not one that already launched. "One shadow per conversation" rides `Session.featureState.planShadow`: the origin records `shadowSessionKey`, the shadow records `shadowOf`. The section is codec-less on purpose — it persists in the sessions snapshot but no codec carries it across a conversation reset, so `/new` legitimately re-arms the review.

**Naming and notice.** The group is named `<origin chat name> · 推敲`, truncated through `truncateGroupName`. The chat's name is its session label unless that is still the generic `defaultSessionName` placeholder, else the name recorded from the chat's messages; a chat with neither takes the `/fork` placeholder, which the existing first-message rename replaces. That is deliberately not `sessionDisplayName`, whose remaining fallbacks (the placeholder, then the raw session key) name the `/list` and `/status` rows — neither belongs in a chat-list entry this feature creates — and deliberately not an unconditional placeholder either, whose rename would be seeded by the review prompt. `SpawnCommonOpts.parentNotice` carries the explanation line onto the origin chat's existing jump card; the notice rides both send paths (card-handle and the `sendAsCardWithButtons` fallback), because applying it to one and not the other silently drops it on platforms without card handles. The shadow chat itself receives no notice message: the name suffix and that jump card are what identify the group to whoever opens it. `/spawn` and `/fork` pass no notice and keep their button-only card. The group also inherits the origin chat's avatar identity: the launch passes the origin's session key as `GroupSpawnOptions.avatarFrom`, and the platform seeds the shadow's registry entry from the origin's — its rendered icon name plus a copy of its cached per-phase image keys — with the origin's `discussing` key as the avatar the creation call carries, so the group is born wearing that face instead of flashing the bot avatar. From there the shadow's own phases paint it as usual (discussing yellow, plan review blue, approved green, closed grey), and a phase the origin has not rendered yet lazy-renders from the copied icon name, so the pair reads as twins. The identity is stable because the naming flow is what hands a spawned group its icon, and a shadow born under `<origin chat name> · 推敲` never enters it: `isSpawnPlaceholderName` refuses the first-message rename, so no later step repaints the face the group was born with. The image keys are the app's own uploads, so the two chats reuse them with no new upload, and the per-phase map is copied rather than aliased; `/spawn` and `/fork` keep their naming path, which is where their icons come from.

**Settlement, both directions.** `settlePlanShadowPeer` is the single entry, called with a trigger naming what happened — `approved`, or `input` for every other user action — and the acting chat's own record says which side of the pair it is, so one call covers both directions and picks its notice from a two-by-two table. `claimPeer` is the one-shot guard: it refuses when the pair section already carries `settled`, and commits `settled` on BOTH records before returning, so two settlements landing in the same tick cannot both pass. `Engine.askUser`'s approval branch calls it with `approved`; the platform ingress calls it with `input`.

- **What counts as acting** (`isUserAction`): text, an attachment, and card buttons (permission verdicts, ask-card answers, follow-ups submissions, command-shortcut buttons) settle the peer. Housekeeping does not — a message starting with `/` and a query button (the `act:`/`nav:` family behind `/list` pagination and a `/status` refresh) are how the user checks on a chat, not how they act on it. Synthetic injections never reach this path at all: a spawn's first message and every machine wake enter `handleMessage` through `receiveMessage`, bypassing the platform ingress. An approving verdict on the chat's own parked plan-review ask is left to that card's settlement (`isPlanCardApproval`), whose notice names the approval and which also covers resolutions that never arrived as a click, so exactly one path settles per action.
- **Closing the peer** goes through the same `closeChat` — `markSpawnedChatDone` → `setChatPhase('done')` → `stopInteractiveSession`, the terminal state committed before the stop so late repaints observe the done mark. The shadow side also leaves a group already carrying its terminal mark alone, and the origin side keeps its two bounds: only a live spawned group is greyed — a main group or a p2p chat owns no avatar axis and is told without any close claimed — while a group already marked done (closed by an earlier settlement, and possibly woken since) voids its card silently. The parked turn is stopped rather than only its ask cancelled, because a cancelled `exit_plan_mode` lets the origin agent plan again and park a competing card, and nothing is exempt for being busy: an ask of any kind parked in the peer, or an unrelated turn, dies with the session — acting in one group wins outright. Like the approval path, the close skips `/done`'s `cmdDone`/`cleanupOneChat`, so the peer's descendant subtask groups and its worktree are untouched and the `feishuBridge/pre-done` waterfall never fires. The notice lands in the peer: a plain card for the shadow group, and for the origin a card carrying the jump button into the shadow.
- **The launch race.** Creating the group takes a moment, and the origin's card can be answered in that window — while the link did not exist yet, so that settlement found no peer to close. `runLaunch` therefore re-reads the origin's park after writing the link and settles a group born after the decision on arrival. A plain message in that window is indistinguishable from no message at all (it changes no state the launch can read), and the origin is still parked on its card then, so the newborn review is kept.

**Retiring an empty-handed review.** A shadow turn that reaches its end without parking a plan card leaves the pair nothing to decide, so `closePlanShadowOnTurnEnd` — called from `Engine.handleResultEvent`'s tail, after that turn's terminal card and baseline repaint, and only for a foreground turn that neither errored nor left queued messages — closes the shadow group through the same `closeChat` the settlement uses: terminal mark, grey avatar, stopped session. Parking a plan-review ask suspends the turn, so a turn that emitted a plan never reaches that tail: reaching it already means no plan card was parked. The rule never touches the origin, whose card stays approvable, and it writes no `settled` marker, so a user who wakes the retired group, gets a new plan out of it, and approves that plan still settles the pair and closes the origin. A pair already settled — the shadow's plan won, or the origin acted — is left alone, as is a group already carrying its terminal mark, so the rule retires each group at most once.

`spawnGroupCommon` returns the synthetic child message (the caller needs the child's session key to link the pair) and accepts the optional parent notice. Every failure path is unchanged and still reports itself in the origin chat, so the shadow path adds no second failure notice.

**Configuration.** `projects[].planShadow.enabled` (default true) and `planShadow.prompt` (default: the wording `plan-shadow.ts` ships, pinned word for word by a test). The prompt is the deployment knob the user asked for: changing what the shadow is asked does not need a rebuild.

## Alternatives considered

- **Auto-submitting the review text as plan feedback in the SAME session.** The cheapest option and closest to what the user does by hand, but it spends the origin card: the whole point is that the original plan stays approvable while the review runs.
- **One shadow per plan card.** Rejected by the user: Feishu cannot delete chats, so every extra card would add a permanent group. The first card of a conversation is also where a second opinion has the most room to change the outcome.
- **Reusing one shadow group across a conversation's later cards.** Avoids group accumulation, but either the group must be re-armed mid-flight or its history mixes reviews of different plans; the "first card only" rule buys the same bound with no re-arming state.
- **Naming the group after `sessionDisplayName`'s fallbacks.** Cheapest, but on a chat that never recorded a name it puts the generic `default` label or the raw session key in the chat list — visible only in the live data (754 of 817 live sessions carry a real name; the rest do not), which is why the case is pinned by a test rather than left to the happy path.
- **Forcing a plan card even when the review finds no improvement.** The user ruled that the agent decides: a forced "improvement" invites make-work. The shadow answers in text when it has nothing better, and that answer retires its own group at the turn's end rather than leaving the review open.
- **Extending `feishuBridge/ask-parked` to plan-review rather than calling the module directly.** That event's existing consumer arms a research-hub ask timeout; a new dispatcher would silently extend that policy to plan asks. A direct call from the ask's own branch, the shape `plan-render` already uses, changes only the plan path.
- **Keying the shadow's identity in the spawned-chat registry (`SpawnedChatMeta`).** The guard that stops recursion reads the asking session's own record, and the engine would have to reach into the platform's store to see it; `Session.featureState` is the session-scoped home, and it is the bag built for exactly this.
- **Deleting a voided shadow group instead of dimming it.** No Feishu API can dissolve a chat, so dimming — with a notice when the peer's action settles it, and without one when the review retires itself — is the whole available lifecycle; dissolving would need an API Feishu does not expose.
- **Closing the origin through `/done`'s own call path (`cmdDone`/`cleanupOneChat`).** The teardown order is the same one `closeChat` reproduces, but `cmdDone` also walks the origin's descendant subtask groups, drains its native children, acts on its worktree (the interactive Keep/Remove card included), and fires the `feishuBridge/pre-done` waterfall other plugins clean state under. A plan card superseded by the shadow's approval has nothing to do with any of those resources, so the close stays local to the origin.
- **Driving the whole rule from the peer's spawned-chat done mark instead of a pair-level one-shot marker.** The mark is the platform's own truth and would cover tracked peers on its own, but a peer the platform does not track (a main group, a p2p chat) never carries one, so every later message there would stop it again and repeat the notice; the marker also makes the two settlement triggers race-free (it commits synchronously, before any await), which reading the mark after the close cannot do.
- **Settling the peer only on an approval, leaving every other action alone.** The shipped behavior before this change; it leaves the user a manual `/done` and lets one side burn work for a decision already made, which is the problem the marker and the ingress entry exist to solve.
- **Reserving the pair before the spawn (`shadowSessionKey` pointing at a not-yet-created group) so an action during creation can void it.** It would also cover the plain-message window, but the reservation is persistent state: a crash between the write and the spawn leaves a group that can never be launched again, and the launch guard reads that same key. Re-reading the park after the spawn keeps the window bounded and leaves no half-state behind.

## Consequences

- **Cost.** Every conversation's first plan card buys one more group, one full transcript fork, and one plan-mode turn — plus a Feishu group the user did not ask for, which the origin chat's jump card explains.
- **Groups accumulate, retired or not.** An empty-handed review is greyed at its turn's end, but no group ever leaves the chat list, and a review that produced a better plan stays live under its own plan. "First card per conversation" bounds it, `/new` resets the counter, and a user who wants none sets `planShadow.enabled: false`.
- **Only a completed turn retires a review.** An errored turn, a turn still holding queued messages, and a background-task turn leave the group open, and so do the turn exits that never reach the result path — a user stop, a stall kill, the hard cap, an agent error event, a channel close with no text: the group then waits for the user, who can retry in it or run `/done`.
- **Race.** A group born while the origin's card was being answered is settled the moment it exists — dimmed, stopped, told — rather than left reviewing a decided plan; the approval window is still never blocked on group creation. Accepted rather than serialized.
- **Unattended plans never spawn one.** The cron synthetic sender is refused; other machine-woken turns keep a real user's chat, and an approver exists there.
- **A closed origin wakes on any message.** The settlement ends the origin's session, not its group: the user sends one message and the chat carries on, the same reactivation `/done`'s grey already allows.
- **An origin the platform does not track is voided, not closed.** A main group or a p2p chat has no avatar axis, so the settlement stops at the card, the parked turn, and the notice; only a live spawned group takes the grey.
- **Acting anywhere in the pair ends the other side's work.** There is no exception for a peer with an unrelated turn running or an ask of another kind parked: the close stops the session, that work included. The user chose this over a quieter close — a pair that stays half-alive is the thing the rule exists to remove.
- **A pair settles once.** After the first settlement neither side closes the other again nor repeats a notice, including when the user wakes the closed group (its avatar returns to the baseline colour on the next message, but the marker stays) and including a peer the platform cannot grey, which a later message would otherwise stop and re-explain every time.
- **The trigger surface is a rule, not a heuristic.** A slash command other than a plain query (`/done` included) and any card button that pushes the conversation count as acting; pagination and refresh buttons do not. A user who runs `/done` in the review group therefore also voids and closes the origin.
- **Avatar inheritance has a floor and no retroactivity.** A shadow whose origin owns no bridge-rendered icon — a main group, a p2p chat, or a chat that never received one — keeps the bot avatar in every phase, exactly as before, and only shadow groups created after the change inherit an identity: an existing shadow group stays on whatever avatar it already wears.
- **Verification.** `packages/acp/feishu-bridge/tests/engine/engine-plan-shadow.spec.ts` drives `Engine.askUser` and `Engine.handleInbound` against a stub spawner: one fork sentinel and plan mode on the child, the group name, the origin chat's notice, and the shadow chat's silence (the launch posts no plain-text message into it), the one-per-conversation rule, the shadow-recursion and cron guards, the launch guards (no plan text, an unknown chat, a session that never started, a chat that is itself a fork, a failed spawn leaving the pair unlinked), the switch, both settlement triggers in both directions (a message in the origin closes the review group with its notice; a message in the review group voids the origin's parked card and closes it, jump button included; an approval keeps its own wording and lands exactly one notice), the trigger surface (a slash command and a query button settle nothing, the review prompt's own injected first message settles nothing — the regression that would close the origin the moment the group is born — and neither does a machine wake), the one-shot rule (later messages, and a woken review group, neither re-close nor re-tell; a review group already carrying its mark is left alone), the origin bounds (a busy origin and one parked on another ask now close with their work, an untracked origin is voided with a notice claiming no close, an already-done origin voids silently, a child group's live state and worktree survive the close), the empty-handed retirement (a shadow turn that ends with no plan card closes the group the `/done` way while the origin card stays approvable; an errored turn, a background-task turn, a turn holding queued messages, an already-settled pair, a group already carrying its mark, and a chat that is not a shadow group all leave it open), the launch race (a group born after the card was answered is closed on arrival), the failure containment (a throwing reply-context reconstruction, a failing dim, and a notice whose jump link cannot be built with the close it follows already committed), and the review prompt word for word — the module at 100% statements, branches, functions, and lines. `tests/assembly-config.spec.ts` pins the config forwarding and its defaults. The spawn path's avatar inheritance is pinned by `packages/acp/feishu-bridge/tests/feishu/spawn-evict.spec.ts`: the source's icon and phase keys copied into the new record, the avatar the creation call carries (with no upload and no avatar update of its own), the bot-avatar floor for an unknown or icon-less source, and the lazy render when the copied record holds no key for the phase yet. Mutation checks turn exactly their own cases red: dropping the one-shot claim, letting a query command settle the pair, reading the injected first message as user input, removing the settlement wiring, removing the empty-handed retirement's wiring, letting the close walk the origin's subtree, dropping the fork-sentinel guard, and removing the fire-and-forget catch (which surfaces as an unhandled error).
- **Deployment.** Bridge rebuild + `/reload`; the feature is on by default, so no config change is required to adopt it.
